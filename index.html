About me

Nice to meet you, I’m Anh!

🗣️ Anh, pronounced “ahn”

I started with watercolors and ended up designing software. Turns out it’s the same instinct: noticing the details and making things feel right.

Painting led me to graphic design, and scrolling way too much social media got me curious about marketing and how products talk to the people who use them.

Product design is where it all clicks. It starts with empathy for the people on the other side of the screen. Then I bring a visual eye and storytelling to complex products, working with product and engineering to make them feel simple, human and genuinely helpful.

Baker Hill · Unify (formerly NextGen)

Giving a lending platform one clear design language

Baker Hill makes loan origination software for community banks and credit unions. Its design pieces were years out of date and never formalized into a system, so every team solved the same problems differently. I helped rebrand NextGen into Unify, rebuild its UI and UX on a shared system, and carry that look into marketing and RISE 2026, Baker Hill's annual lending conference.

Role
Product Designer
Timeline
[Start] – present
Team
Product, engineering, marketing, sales
Tools
Figma, [others]
The starting point

A system that had fallen behind

NextGen had grown feature by feature. Screens built years apart used different buttons, tables and spacing, so lenders had to relearn the product every time they moved between steps. The components that did exist were outdated and undocumented, so designers and engineers kept rebuilding the same pieces.

Before: NextGen's loan workflow and CRM.

Rebrand

From NextGen to Unify

"NextGen" described a moment in time. "Unify" describes the promise: one workflow from application to close. I helped shape the new identity and applied it across the product, social and sales materials so customers saw one brand everywhere.

Social media

The new look, in people's feeds

I also design Baker Hill's social graphics, so the brand shows up the same way in a LinkedIn feed as it does in the product.

Design system

One set of parts for dense lending data

Loan origination is tables, long forms and status at every step. I built the shared foundations first (color and type variables, text styles and a 12-column grid), then the components lenders touch most: data tables, form fields, filters and status chips. Now new features start from one up-to-date system instead of old one-off screens.

UI + UX overhaul

Clearer screens, on every device

With the system in place, I redesigned core flows like opportunities and the loan application so the next action is always obvious. Light and dark themes, plus tablet and mobile layouts, let loan officers work the way they already do: at a desk, in a branch or with a borrower.

Cross-team features

AskBKR: answers without leaving the workflow

I partnered with product and engineering on features across the platform. One was AskBKR, an AI assistant built into Unify, so lenders can ask about a policy or a loan without opening another tool. Suggested prompts help first-time users know what to ask.

RISE 2026

Bringing it to the customers

RISE is Baker Hill's annual lending conference. I worked with marketing, sales and product to design the event's experience and materials around the new Unify story. The result: more deals won and a strong NPS from attendees.

Rally · iOS app · 2024

Turning the urge to volunteer into a booked shift

Rally helps people in Dallas–Fort Worth find and schedule local volunteering. Existing sites list opportunities but leave out the logistics busy people need before committing, so I led a mobile design built around at-a-glance details and one-tap booking.

Role
Lead Product Designer
Timeline
Design sprint, Jan – Apr 2024
Team
Emma Wilkie (PM), Bri Castillo, Savannah Georgecink, Nhi Vo (Product), Morgan Goerke (Visual)
Tools
Figma, Adobe Illustrator
The problem

People want to help. Then they don't sign up.

There's a large gap between intending to volunteer and actually doing it. Current platforms work like bulletin boards and lack the scheduling busy professionals need to commit with confidence.

  • Business need: centralize opportunities to raise community engagement and reduce friction for local nonprofits.
  • User need: clear, at-a-glance schedules and requirements so intent becomes action.
Research

The barrier was uncertainty, not interest

50–70%of volunteers quit within their first year

We paired industry data on the $3.7B volunteer management market with small, deep-dive interviews. People dropped off when a listing didn't say how long it would take or what to expect on the day: parking, gear, tasks.

So every opportunity card answers who, what, where and when at a glance.

Competitors

Big databases, then an "email wall"

Platforms like VolunteerMatch have huge nonprofit databases but favor quantity over clarity. Signing up usually means manual email back-and-forth, which turns a quick yes into a multi-day wait.

How might we help Dallas residents understand what local shelters need, and make volunteering or donating feel seamless?
Process

Must-know details first

The user flow puts parking, attire and task descriptions up front, cutting the back-and-forth that causes drop-off. Volunteers reach "Commit" already knowing what they're signing up for.

We sketched fast, with everyone contributing ideas in short rounds, then moved to mid-fidelity so senior designers and developers could react early. Testing logistics-heavy layouts as functional mocks let us refine navigation in real time.

The solution

From discovery to day-of, in one place

  • Curated feed. Opportunities ranked by your interests, location and real-time urgency.
  • Advanced filters. Narrow by skill, cause and availability.
  • One-tap booking. Secure a shift without the email wall.
Outcome

A directory became a retention tool

Reviewers rated the final designs highly for reducing friction and bringing clarity to a cluttered space. Solving the email wall and surfacing logistics up front turned a fragmented, manual process into one that respects volunteers' time.

GOBankingRates · 2024

Making money advice feel approachable on social

GOBankingRates shares financial advice, tips and stories. I helped strengthen its brand presence and build a social strategy that paired engaging visuals with relatable money insights.

Role
Design Assistant Intern
Timeline
May – Oct 2024
Team
Design, Marketing, Social Media
Tools
Figma, Meta Business Suite
53%more audience reach
84K+impressions
50%higher story engagement
Strategy

How do we connect more closely with followers?

I set up a tight feedback loop with the social leads and co-created content pillars in FigJam. Then I turned audience insights into flexible templates that stayed on-brand but could react quickly to money news.

Gameplan

Let the data pick the formats

We studied how our audience behaved and which posts performed, to find the visual formats that resonated. I helped build a content calendar that kept design and social output steady.

Design

Visualizing financial life stages

I made custom icons, type hierarchies and chart styles that felt authoritative but friendly.

  • Actionable advice. High-contrast "Daily Tips" layouts that stay legible on small phones.
  • Data storytelling. Simplified charts that make market trends easy to digest.
  • Human-centric. Photography that reflects the community's different life stages.
Podcast

"What the Why": from one-way content to a conversation

Listeners send in money questions that the hosts answer live. I designed the podcast's landing page around discovery and conversion, so people can listen, subscribe and submit a question in one place, and kept it consistent across social.

See the live page

I also built Instagram templates from the brand's core elements, so the social team could turn episode highlights and guest quotes into short-form posts quickly.
